01 / Overview

Turning fragmented indicators into one consistent monitoring view.

Indonesian macroeconomic and consumer indicators are published across different datasets, frequencies, and release schedules. Bringing them into one monitoring view requires consistent treatment of time periods, units, aggregation rules, and missing data.

I built this project to simulate a recurring analytical workflow for a strategy or business analytics team that needs to monitor monetary conditions, inflation, exchange-rate movements, consumer confidence, and retail activity in one place.

The final workflow connects raw source files, Power Query transformations, monthly analytical metrics, executive commentary, and an automated Excel dashboard.

05 / Analytical Decisions

The important part was not just cleaning the data.

BI RATE

Represent monthly policy using the latest decision.

When multiple policy decisions occurred within the same month, I retained the final decision to represent the prevailing policy stance at month-end rather than averaging policy-rate events.

JISDOR

Aggregate daily exchange-rate observations consistently.

Daily JISDOR observations were converted into monthly averages. Partial September 2026 data was excluded to avoid comparing incomplete periods with fully observed months.

PUBLICATION LAG

Preserve publication lags instead of filling missing periods.

Consumer confidence and retail data were not forward-filled. Each KPI retains its own latest published period so the dashboard reflects actual data availability.

INTERPRETATION

Separate numerical movement from economic interpretation.

For JISDOR, a higher USD/IDR level means a weaker rupiah. The dashboard therefore distinguishes the direction of the metric from the economic meaning of that movement.

06 / Output

Designed for monitoring, not just visualization.

The final dashboard brings five indicators into a single monitoring view using KPI cards, trend charts, and an executive readout. Each KPI shows its own latest available period so publication lags remain visible rather than being hidden behind a common reporting date.

The visual layer is designed to make interpretation easier. Consumer Confidence retains the 100-point optimism threshold, Retail Sales Growth distinguishes positive and negative growth, and JISDOR is interpreted using the convention that a higher USD/IDR level represents a weaker rupiah.

Power Query handles the underlying data transformation, while VBA is used for dashboard generation, refresh automation, and presentation. This separation keeps the analytical pipeline traceable while allowing the final output to remain practical for recurring monthly monitoring.

07 / Key Insights

What the latest data suggests.

MONETARY POLICY

BI Rate held at 5.75% in August 2026.

The policy rate was unchanged from July, while remaining 0.75 percentage points above its August 2025 level.

INFLATION

Inflation rose to 3.19% YoY.

August inflation increased by 0.31 percentage points from the previous month and was 0.88 percentage points higher than twelve months earlier.

EXCHANGE RATE

Monthly rupiah pressure eased, but the annual comparison remained weaker.

Average JISDOR declined by around 1.0% MoM in August, indicating monthly rupiah strengthening. However, USD/IDR remained about 9.3% higher than twelve months earlier.

CONSUMER PULSE

Confidence remained optimistic while retail growth stayed negative.

Consumer Confidence stood at 116.8 in July, remaining above the 100-point optimism threshold. Retail Sales Growth was -3.0% YoY in June despite improving from the previous month.

The indicators are released on different publication schedules, so each observation above uses its latest available period. June 2026 is the latest month with complete observations across all five indicators and should be used for direct cross-indicator comparison.

I also tested whether the VBA refresh workflow continued to work after reopening the workbook, so the final dashboard remained usable as a recurring monthly monitoring tool rather than a one-time static report.

09 / What I Learned

A dashboard is only the visible end of the work.

This project reinforced that reliable analysis starts before the chart. Each indicator required a clear definition of frequency, unit, publication timing, aggregation method, and interpretation before it could be combined into one dashboard.

I also learned to separate the workflow into distinct layers: source data, Power Query transformation, analytical logic, and presentation. This made the process easier to validate, troubleshoot, refresh, and explain to another user.

Most importantly, the project showed me that a useful dashboard is not defined by the number of charts it contains, but by whether the data, metrics, and commentary consistently answer the questions the dashboard was designed to monitor.
